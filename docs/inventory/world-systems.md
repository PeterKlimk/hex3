# World systems inventory

Status: working inventory, based on the Rust code as of 2026-07-12. This describes what the code does, not what older design documents say it was intended to do. Fidelity labels are provisional assessments for discussion, not verdicts.

## Reading the fidelity labels

- **Physically based**: state, units, conservation, or causal relationships retain physical meaning, despite major simplification.
- **Authentic hack**: an engineered shortcut aims to reproduce characteristic consequences and correlations without solving the governing system.
- **Visual/procedural hack**: generated texture or shape mainly serves appearance. This is not inherently undesirable, but should remain explicit.
- **Simulation-grade** is not assigned to any complete subsystem here. Several solvers use respectable numerical methods, but the boundary conditions, forcing, calibration, and validation do not support quantitative planetary prediction.

## Actual stage graph

```text
Stage 0  coarse spherical tessellation
  ↓
Stage 1  plates + independent crust + Euler motion
         → boundary episodes/features/deformation experiments
         → coarse elevation
  ↓
Stage 2  temperature + prescribed circulation/wind
         → moisture transport + precipitation
  ↓
Stage 3  adaptive fine tessellation and transferred fields
         → synthesized structural substrate
         → pre-erosion hydrology
  ↓
Stage 4  fine fluvial/hillslope erosion (+ optional experiments)
         → lapse/orographic climate adjustment
         → final hydrology
```

`World` retains both stage-3 and stage-4 fine surfaces. `view_stage` is a presentation cap over already computed state, not a simulation rollback. Despite older module comments calling erosion future work, stage 4 is implemented and is the headless default.

## Important model variants and disabled/partial work

### Orogen models

`OrogenModel` contains 13 paths: legacy, several conserved footprints/diffusion choices, thin-sheet and yield variants, three history-aware variants, and carrier-evolved/lifecycle variants. The code deliberately keeps `Legacy` as the product default. Therefore the repository contains considerably more tectonic machinery than a normal run exercises. Any architecture document must distinguish “implemented and selectable” from “accepted product model.”

The carrier/lifecycle path is the closest attempt at explicit material evolution: it tracks continental/oceanic material, ocean age, sutures, underthrusting, arc magma, remap overlap/gaps, foundering/export, and present uplift rate. It is also experimental, coarse, parameterized, and expensive. Its conservation audits establish internal consistency, not geological truth.

### Erosion experiments

The runtime surface exposes many experiments whose defaults may be zero/off: nonlinear hillslopes, MFD, alluvial confinement, deposition, uplift smoothing, lithologic/structural erodibility, emergent orogen rebuild, meso structure, drainage-aware uplift pulse, multiple precipitation-feedback passes, lake evaporation, and glacial abrasion. They should not be catalogued as simultaneously active features without checking current constants and CLI overrides for a given run.

### Explicitly unfinished or absent

- Glacial valley widening is explicitly deferred; ice sheets and glaciers are not dynamically simulated.
- Wetland classification is a hydrology TODO.
- No biome/ecology stage exists, despite old module comments naming it as future work.
- No sediment stratigraphy, sediment transport network, coastal transport/deltas as evolving landforms, groundwater, snowpack, groundwater-fed rivers, or water chemistry.
- No seasonal/orbital cycle, coupled ocean circulation, sea ice, dynamic radiation budget, storms, or weather variability.
- No solved global sea level/water inventory, flexural-isostatic response to erosion/sediment/ice, or coastline migration through geological time.
- Default plates and continents do not emerge from the tectonic history; lifecycle experiments partially address this but are not the product path.
- Plate motion lacks mantle/force coupling, and reorganization histories are prescribed stochastic timelines.
- Hydrology and erosion operate on static tectonic geometry during an erosion run; only parameterized uplift couples tectonics to denudation.
- Physical dimensional provenance is uneven: some rates use km/Myr and extensive solid-angle quantities, while temperature, elevation and precipitation are often normalized worldbuilding units.

## Substantive coupling gaps

These are observations, not yet roadmap recommendations.

1. **Tectonics ↔ surface processes:** erosion removes/redistributes material, but the accepted product tectonic/elevation solution does not respond through unloading, sediment loading, flexure or evolving plate/crust state. Experimental uplift and lifecycle ledgers provide hooks.
2. **Climate ↔ water surface:** stage-2 evaporation sources are simply cells below sea level; lakes appear later. Stage 4 adds only a local lake-humidity shortcut. There is no full climate rerun after hydrology changes the surface-water map.
3. **Climate ↔ erosion:** fine orographic feedback is sensibly Pareto-limited but cannot alter planetary circulation or temperature beyond local lapse correction.
4. **Ocean age ↔ crust/bathymetry:** ridge distance/opening rate drive an age proxy and thermal subsidence, but ocean crust is not continuously created, advected and consumed in the default world.
5. **Sediment continuity:** erosion tracks deposition and loss ledgers, but sediment is not a persistent material layer feeding lithology, basin fill, deltas, coastlines or tectonic recycling.
6. **Scale contract:** adaptive fine resolution improves visible terrain and drainage but makes physical thresholds, convergence and presentation sensitive to discretization. Considerable area/distance normalization exists; systematic cross-resolution validation is present in tooling intent but not a universal test contract.

## Compute-versus-benefit observations

- The coarse/fine split is the central Pareto decision: tectonics and global climate remain tractable on the coarse graph; rivers and erosion receive millions of adaptive cells.
- The fine base is expensive enough to warrant an authoritative disk cache, parallel loops, kd-trees, compact adjacency and lazy area caching. Erosion variants deliberately reuse it.
- Moisture uses a steady single-layer finite-volume solve rather than 3-D circulation; fine climate uses local modulation rather than a global rerun. Both are high-leverage simplifications.
- Carrier lifecycle tectonics and repeated erosion–precipitation passes have roughly time-step/pass-proportional costs. They need stronger visible/emergent benefits than default legacy fields to justify product adoption.
- Many small shape systems in fine synthesis add conceptual and tuning cost even when computationally cheap. Their justification should be based on whether erosion preserves and organizes their signal, not merely whether they can generate it.
