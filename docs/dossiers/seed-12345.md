# Planet dossier: seed 12345

Status: **preliminary causal and numerical autopsy; matched spatial inspection
pending**, 2026-07-12.

This is the first end-to-end application of the [project thesis](../thesis.md)
and [model strategy](../model-strategy.md) to one generated world. It asks
whether seed 12345 contains coherent geographic situations, why they exist, how
presentation changes their appearance, and which mechanisms earn their place.

It deliberately does not infer geographic objects from global distributions.
The present evidence supports several strong mechanism and object-level
findings, but no complete visual verdict because the reference corpus contains
no images or full spatial export.

## World character currently supported by evidence

At 1M cells seed 12345 has 25.97% land, median land elevation 0.758 km,
90th/99th-percentile land elevation 1.220/3.984 km, and a 9.05 km maximum. It
has the strongest short-scale relief in the ten-seed reference panel: sampled
10-km relief p90 is 1,324 m and 25-km relief p90 is 2,878 m.

The correct provisional description is therefore **a strongly dissected or
short-wavelength mountainous world**, not merely a tall world. Whether that
structure forms convincing ranges, plateaus, valleys and basins remains only
partly established.

The former apparent pillar is retained as a presentation warning. The 250k
spire probe finds gradual high-terrain support around the top summits and passes
its physical spire gate. Yet the same terrain reaches apparent median outer
flank angles up to 38 degrees under the diagnostic relief transform. A rendered
tower can therefore be a cartographic amplification of supported terrain rather
than a physical spike.

## What actually generates this world

The accepted terrain lineage is:

```text
procedural plate and craton regions
  -> random rigid Euler motions
  -> present-boundary interaction fields
  -> prescribed arc/collision/ridge/trench/rift response profiles
  -> Airy/thermal/flexural elevation plus authored craton relief
  -> sea-level shift targeting 26% land
  -> adaptive fine interpolation
  -> precipitation-weighted stream-power incision + hillslope diffusion
  -> repeated legacy tectonic hold/rebuild source
  -> deposition/sink ledger and final basin-integration cuts
  -> final elevation, lakes and river topology
```

This is an authentic systemic terrain generator, not a geological
reconstruction. It preserves spherical plate kinematics, boundary sign and
polarity, broad tectonic setting, isostatic/thermal structure, drainage routing
and a causal landscape operator. Absolute amplitudes, belt widths, craton
relief, land fraction, numerical maturity and several drainage repairs are
strong authored priors.

### Important mismatch: paid-for history without inherited terrain

The product path unconditionally computes `TectonicHistory` and physical-rate
work/flux fields, but `OrogenModel::Legacy` does not consume that history to
generate elevation. Its terrain is driven by present boundary response fields.
The project is therefore paying some history complexity without receiving
readable inherited geography from it.

### Important mismatch: tectonic response is used twice

Legacy arc and collision response first becomes static coarse elevation through
tectonic thickness. During erosion, equivalent `tectonic_thickening` is added
again as a source for each of 200 dimensionless steps. This is best understood
as a calibrated **hold-and-carve servo**: prescribed mountains are rebuilt while
routed water carves them. It is not tectonic uplift and erosion on a shared
geological clock.

That architecture may still be an excellent authentic hack. It earns retention
only if it creates better range, valley, divide and basin organization than a
cheaper drainage-conditioned terrain model.

### What is not responsible

The reference configuration has fault scarps, fine interior relief, strike
bands, margin contrast, emergent rebuild, meso relief, lithologic texture,
nonlinear hillslopes, MFD, climate feedback, lake evaporation and glaciers
disabled. Those experimental branches cannot explain this world's current
strengths or failures.

## Provisional object findings

### Mountain systems

The 250k probe identifies 120 high-mountain components and 14 significant
components above 20,000 km². Significant elongation has p10/p50/p90 of
1.9/3.8/6.5: there are belt-like objects, but the population is much blobbier
than a consistently linear orogenic grammar.

Several components are enormous. The largest is about 3.0 million km² and
4,839 × 620 km; the next two are about 1.5 and 1.4 million km². The p90 area
within 0.5 km of a range summit is 19,724 km², and p90 low-grade coverage within
that cap is 53%. This is evidence of broad summit/high-surface structure mixed
with the strong relief—not a world composed only of narrow alpine crests.

Erosion is a primary morphology owner. At sampled mountain cells, pre-erosion
25/50/100-km p90 max-minus-min relief is 256/604/1,307 m; after erosion it is
1,520/2,897/4,263 m. It does much more than polish a tectonic surface. It also
raises pit and peak counters, so increased relief is not automatically improved
landform organization.

The current audit cannot yet answer whether the largest components follow
convergent fronts, whether broad caps are defensible plateaus, or whether
drainage divides form stable range skeletons. Those require spatial object
ancestry and matched views.

### River network

The 250k catchment-ranked network has maximum Strahler order 4 and bifurcation
ratio 8.1, compared with the audit's terrestrial prior of roughly 3–5. Its
drainage density is 0.0033 km/km², below the broad Earth map-scale reference of
0.01–0.1. The legacy count-equivalent selection is sparser still.

The longest reported trunk is 2,173 km on a continent with 10,373 km maximum
extent. Reported top profiles are inconsistent: one drops only 13% in its upper
half and another reports a negative upper-half drop. There are 2,067 ocean
mouths and maximum order remains low, suggesting fragmented selection or basin
hierarchy rather than a few continent-organizing networks.

These are not sufficient grounds to tune a river threshold. The first questions
are whether basin identities are coherent, whether erosion organized valleys
and captures, and whether semantic reach construction is representing the
authoritative drainage graph faithfully.

### Lakes and basins

Lakes are the clearest object-level concern. At 250k, final fine lakes occupy
4.71% of land versus 2.46% in the coarse preview; the 1M corpus reports 3.30%.
This budget dependence alone prevents a stable product claim.

The 250k final surface has 13 threshold lakes totaling 6.64 million km². The
largest is 2.60 million km² and the next two are 1.20 and 1.04 million km².
Catchment/lake ratios are only 3/4/9× at p10/p50/p90, below the audit's broad
10–100× prior, while lake-surface land-hypsometry percentiles rise to 15/51/93.
This describes extremely large lakes, some occupying surprisingly high parts of
the land distribution, with small contributing areas relative to their size.

The wetness dial changes lake area without recomputing precipitation, river
flow or erosion. Lake climate can therefore move independently of the rest of
the water system. Final basin integration also carves outlet notches and those
modified elevations become authoritative terrain. A plausible water gap may be
a retained repair rather than an emergent erosional crossing.

This does not establish that all lakes are wrong. It establishes that lake
storage/equilibrium, resolution survival and repair provenance need examination
before lake abundance can be presented as physical correspondence.

### Climate and moisture

Seed 12345's reference mean relative aridity is 1.858, but a world mean cannot
validate climate geography. The accepted model has a real reduced core:
prescribed spherical circulation, terrain-modified and projected wind, and
finite-volume moisture transport with ocean source, rainout, recycling and
mixing.

Its output is a normalized relative climatology. Precipitation is rescaled to a
fixed land mean and treated directly as runoff; no physical discharge, soil,
groundwater, snow, seasons or storage exists. Fine atmosphere is interpolated
from the coarse solution. In the reference configuration fine orographic
precipitation, downwind shadow, feedback and lake evaporation are all off.

There is also a source mismatch: climate treats every below-sea cell as an
ocean evaporation source before hydrology later applies stricter ocean
connectivity. A sub-sea inland depression can therefore influence moisture as
ocean before becoming a lake or dry basin.

No current spatial evidence shows whether the expensive circulation/projection/
transport chain creates more coherent rain shadows, basin runoff or river
importance than a cheaper latitude–elevation–coast-distance climatology.

### Coast and presentation

There is not yet a stable coast hierarchy. Shoreline is a zero-elevation
contour after a land-fraction datum shift and final hydrologic terrain repair;
it is not a water-volume solution or coastal-process model. Adaptive ocean
coarsening and cell contours may affect islands, straits and river mouths.

Rendering preserves authoritative state but applies large, legitimate
cartographic transformations. Rivers are selected by catchment rules, baked
into an equirectangular distance field and reconstructed at screen-space width;
lake outflows receive forced-major styling rather than width from modeled
overflow discharge. Default terrain "moisture" greening is actually
flow-accumulation styling, so the normal terrain view cannot validate the
climate field.

Matched Physical, Diagnostic, Cartographic and Dramatic captures do not yet
exist with a complete transform ledger. Visual appeal, range legibility, coast
character and the pillar regression therefore remain open evidence—not failed
or passed gates.

## The first decisive evidence packet

The next work is not parameter tuning. For seed 12345, select at least:

1. one high-relief belt;
2. one broad summit or plateau candidate;
3. one major basin and trunk river;
4. one large terminal or overflowing lake;
5. one coast/island/strait complex;
6. one expected precipitation gradient;
7. one visibly odd counterexample.

For each object record:

```text
identity and scale
  -> authoritative measurements and confidence
  -> proposed causal fields and conditional expectation
  -> pre/fine/pre-erosion/final ancestry
  -> hydrologic-repair provenance
  -> downstream consumers
  -> Physical / Diagnostic / Cartographic / Dramatic captures
  -> correspondence verdict
  -> cheaper substitute verdict
```

The minimum matched capture packet should include true-scale relief, current
cartographic relief with its scale, hillshade/diagnostic elevation, plate
boundaries and convergence, pre/post-erosion terrain, drainage/basin hierarchy,
lake outlets and repair cuts, precipitation/wind, and final terrain. Seed,
revision, stage, camera, viewport, relief scale, river profile and all overlay
settings must accompany it.

## Current verdict

Seed 12345 demonstrates that Hex3 can generate abundant relief and that erosion
meaningfully reorganizes terrain. It does **not yet demonstrate** that this
relief forms coherent, causally placed geographic systems.

The strongest present concerns are not peak height. They are:

- broad/blobby mountain ownership and the legacy hold-and-carve composition;
- weak or fragmented river hierarchy and questionable profiles;
- enormous, high-standing and resolution-sensitive lakes;
- hidden terrain mutation by hydrologic integration;
- climate complexity without demonstrated object-level downstream advantage;
- presentation capable of making supported terrain appear pathological.

The appropriate next intervention is therefore semantic/spatial inspection and
causal comparison, not tuning erosion, adding ecology, or promoting a deeper
tectonic model.
